Ranked and bots

Ranked matches players against bots: AI opponents with a deck, a search strength and a name. Each bot has a rating measured by having the bots play each other, so the ladder knows who is stronger before anyone plays them.

What a bot is

  • A deck from either career/campaigns/decks/<format>/ or the dedicated ranked pool in ladder/decks/<format>/ (100% Rust-verified and format-legal).
  • One of the AI strengths the Rust engine plays: quick answers (skill 1), a fifth of the search (3), half (5) or the full search (7). Higher-rank Generation 2 bots add declarative AI Playbooks (ladder/playbooks.json / playmat-ai/src/playbooks.json) for archetype profiles, mana hold-up discipline, modal/discard/combat hints, wider search budgets, and Bo3 sideboard plans.
  • A persona: a name, avatar, sleeves, playmat, frame and title from the cosmetics catalog. Stronger bots wear rarer cosmetics.

Standard fields 40 unique Open decks (98 bots), Pioneer 42 unique Open decks (102 bots), Modern 30 unique Open decks (58 bots, Open and Bo3 queues only), Commander 31 unique 100-card decks (62 bots across 1v1 Duel, 4-Player Pod, and League), Pauper 23 unique commons-only decks (46 bots across Bo1, Bo3, and League), Brawl 21 unique 60-card singleton decks (42 bots across 1v1 and League), and Legacy 21 unique eternal decks (42 bots across Bo1 and Bo3). Each format is its own rating scale, with its starter deck at skill 1 fixed at 1000.

Each bot keeps the search settings and playbook it was rated with, so a stronger AI in a later release doesn't change how existing bots play or what they're rated. A stronger AI comes in as a new generation of bots, rated against the old ones with --fixed. Old bots can be retired: they're no longer met, but past games still show who they were. Updating a server needs nothing for this.

Where they live

  • Mage.Server/src/main/resources/ladder/bots.json lists the bots and their measured ratings. It ships with the server.
  • db/ladder.db keeps ratings as they change with play: every player's and bot's rating per format and queue. It is in the nightly backup. Deleting it puts players back at 1200 and bots back at their measured ratings.

Ranked

Ranked is in Career (Career › Ranked). It is on wherever Career is: signed-in accounts on a server with sign-in turned on. There is nothing else to set up.

  • Each format has its own ladder: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum and Diamond, with four tiers of four pips each, then Mythic. A win is a pip. Up to Gold, a third win in a row is two. Losses cost nothing in Bronze. From Silver up they cost a pip, but never drop you below the tier (Silver) or the rank (Gold and up).
  • Opponents are picked from distinct decks near the player's hidden rating. The pick is persisted in db/ladder.db until played so it can't be dodged, and its exact deck stays hidden until the match starts (an Expected Field forecast is shown instead). After every match -- especially a loss -- the matchmaker rotates to a different deck and puts lost decks on cooldown so players never get stuck on one adversary.
  • Ranked games are best of one, with no hints, take-backs, practice tools, saves or what-ifs. Conceding or leaving is a loss. A game the server loses in a restart doesn't count. Finished matches unlock post-game inspection of the opponent's 60+15 decklist.
  • The Bo3 switch on the Ranked screen opens a second queue: best of three against the same bots, with sideboarding between games for both the player and Playbook-equipped AI bots (using only cards publicly revealed in earlier games). It has its own rank and hidden rating. Its ranks unlock the same rewards as best of one, once for both. Leaving between games is a loss like any other.
  • Commander Ranked offers both 1v1 Duel and 4-Player Pod queues. In 4-Player Pods, the server seats three distinct AI Commander decks near your rating and scores exact finish order: 1st place awards +2 pips, 2nd awards +1 pip in Bronze through Gold (safe in Platinum+), 3rd is safe in Bronze through Gold (-1 in Platinum and Diamond), and 4th costs 1 pip from Silver up. Bronze and Silver include anti-combo Bracket Protection that keeps high-power meta decks out until Gold.
  • The Limited rank moves with Career's sealed and draft runs (Career › Sealed & draft), not with a queue. When a run ends, it moves by the run's wins less its losses, in pips, with no streak bonus. A bad run costs one pip at most, with the same floors as above. Leaving a run after its first game counts it with the games left as losses. A cube drafted from your own deck doesn't count. It has its own seasons, boards and rank rewards, but no Mythic rating.
  • Reaching a rank for the first time unlocks its title, sleeve and frame. Gold, Diamond and Mythic also pay a capsule token. The usual Career XP, quests and daily rewards apply as in any other game.

Standings, games and the ranks paid are kept in db/ladder.db. An account's export includes them, and deleting an account deletes them.

Seasons

Ranked and the League run in two-month seasons, the same for every format. The first starts on 1 November 2026 (UTC). Set XMAGE_SEASON_START (a date) and XMAGE_SEASON_MONTHS (1 to 24) in ops/web/.env to change the calendar. Changing it later never takes the ladder back a season.

  • A new season places each player lower: Mythic and Diamond to Platinum 4, Platinum to Gold 4, Gold to Silver 4, Silver to Bronze 1. Bronze stays where it is. Hidden ratings carry over, so strong players climb back fast.
  • Rank cosmetics and capsule tokens are earned once per account, in any season. Nothing is exclusive to a season; past seasons' best ranks show as stamps on the standing.
  • The League's packs and wildcards for ranks and tiers pay again each season, and the end of a season pays League packs by the best rank: 1 for Bronze up to 6 and 2 mythic wildcards for Mythic. They are set in league/league.json.
  • A game counts for the season it started in.
  • Each format, queue and season has three boards on the Ranked and League screens: peak rank, Mythic rating, and fastest to Mythic (fewest games). Past seasons keep theirs. They are read straight from db/ladder.db, so there is nothing to set up; a deleted account leaves them. Home has a Season board widget.

League

The League (Career › League) is Ranked played with the cards an account owns. It is on wherever Career is.

  • A player starts a 60-card format's League by picking two of its starter decks, or starts the Commander Precon Upgrade League by picking one 100-card precon plus six Commander and expansion packs. Their cards join the Career collection (never past four of a card), each becomes a League deck, and the format's packs are added. Commander League decks enforce singleton rules and can be queued in either 1v1 Duel or 4-Player Pod tables. The account's first League also gives 4 common, 3 uncommon and 1 rare wildcard.
  • League packs come from the format's own sets. Standard picks a set; Pioneer also has a mixed pack. A pack uses the format's League packs first, then a Career pack token, then 100 coins. Every fourth League pack also gives a rare wildcard, and every twelfth a mythic one instead.
  • League decks must be legal in the format and use only cards the account owns. The server plays its own copy of the deck. In the League's deck builder a deck can hold any card legal in the format, owned or not; Craft missing crafts everything it lacks at once, or nothing if the wildcards or coins don't cover it.
  • The League has its own ladder, with the same ranks and rules as Ranked, starting at a lower hidden rating. Each tier reached for the first time pays a pack of the format. Each rank pays its cosmetics and a rare wildcard (mythic at Gold). Every fifth League win pays an uncommon wildcard. No capsule tokens.
  • League opponents are picked by rating, like Ranked's. The League adds budget versions of the meta decks (rares and mythics swapped for commons and uncommons) and plays the full meta decks only at the full search. The budget decks rate well below their full lists, so they turn up among the starters and the school's decks.
  • The pace is tested: a player with 6 League games a day who puts spare coins into League packs finishes a Standard meta deck in about four weeks (a median of 27 days). At 2 games a day it takes about three months.

The starters, the number of packs and the wildcard track are set in league/league.json in the server's resources. Leagues, League packs and League decks are kept in db/career.db and the League's ladder in db/ladder.db. Both are in an account's export and are deleted with it.

Recalibrate

The ratings in bots.json are only right for the AI that measured them. After changing the AI's search, the skills or a school deck, measure them again. The shipped ratings come from about 31,000 games on three threads: 40 minutes for the first 18,000, about 20 more for the League's budget bots.

  1. If you added or renamed school decks, remake the list: 
    python3 Mage.Server/ladder-src/roster.py
    Bots that keep their id keep their persona and rating. If you changed a meta deck, remake its budget version first: 
    mvn -o -pl Mage.Server test -Dtest=LeagueBudgetDecksTest -Dxmage.writeBudgetDecks=true
  2. Play the bots against each other and write the ratings back: 
    cd rust
cargo run --release -p playmat-ai --bin ladder-calibrate -- --rounds 3 --near 8 --far 3 --deals 6
    Run it with the same PLAYMAT_AI_POLICY as your server (the default is playouts). A run can stop and pick up where it left off.
  3. Rebuild the server image so it ships the new bots.json.
